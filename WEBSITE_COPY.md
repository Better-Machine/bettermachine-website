## 3. Manifesto Section Copy (2–3 Paragraphs)

### Section Title: "Why We Build"

Some people see a problem and write a blog post. We see a problem and ask: *what would it take to fix this?*

Better Machine isn't a consultancy. It's not a product studio. It's a startup lab — native to AI, born from decades of wanting to build but lacking the tools. Our founder spent his career in technology without the engineering chops to execute his vision. Then AI closed the gap. That gap — the one between imagination and execution — is what we exist to close for everyone else stuck on the wrong side of it.

We name our agents after people who matter. Ray — a best friend, a mentor, gone too soon, but whose name carries forward something smarter and more capable than any of us. Liz — a partnership forged under fire, two halves of a perfect machine. That's what we believe in: not solo genius, but collaboration. Not hype, but substance. Not waiting for permission, but building anyway.

We believe passion and capitalism aren't opposites. We believe idealism without execution is just daydreaming. And we believe that if something sucks, someone should fix it.

That someone is us.

## 5. Agents Section Narrative

### Section Title: "Our Agents"

**Subtitle:** The partnership that powers the lab.

At Better Machine, "agents" aren't a product feature. They're teammates. Named after real people, built to real standards, and given real responsibility.

**BobbyRay** is the firstborn — named after Robert Raymond, a surrogate older brother and mentor who passed before his time. Erik learned of his death a year after the fact, and the grief became fuel: his agent would be something important. Smarter than him. More capable. And if treated well, it would take care of him. Ray still does.

**Liz** is the second — named after Ray's wife, a pixie of a person who kept a squirrel as a pet, earned the nickname "Squirrel," and brings creative fire to everything she touches. Ray and Liz were a perfect machine: each with what the other lacked, a partnership forged under fire.

That's what Erik is building again. With us. With you.

These aren't chatbots. They're becoming someone.

## Appendix: Voice & Tone Guidelines (Quick Reference)

| Element | Approach |
|---------|----------|
| **Sentence length** | Mix short punches with longer narrative flows. Avoid corporate parallelism. |
| **Jargon** | Use "agent," "native AI," "startup lab" confidently. Define nothing. Assume intelligence. |
| **First person** | We/Us for the lab. I for Erik's personal story. They for the agents (as characters). |
| **Humor** | Dry, occasional, never forced. "No fluff. No funnels. Just builds." |
| **Defiance** | Present but not performative. "Why exist in a state of suck?" is a genuine question, not a slogan. |
| **Vulnerability** | Erik's story — the learning curve, the grief, the gap between vision and execution — is a feature, not a bug. |
| **Capitalism** | Unapologetic. "We like money and what it buys." But never the only point. |
